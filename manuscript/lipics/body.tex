\section{Introduction}\label{introduction}

A fault-injection scheduler determines which behaviors a
distributed-system test can observe. Twins, randomized Byzantine
testing, Netrix and ByzzBench demonstrate the practical value of
controlling message delivery
\cite{bano2021twins,winter2023randomized,dragoi2024netrix,louroneto2025byzzbench}.
These controls expose a design problem: \textbf{which distinctions
between admission decisions are necessary to observe an agreement
violation, and what is their minimum cost?} A phase-specific policy may
admit an enabling prevote and suppress a later precommit carrying repair
evidence. A single admission bit for their shared peer/view key cannot
make that choice. Additional evidence can repair a validator before it
signs an indispensable conflicting vote.

Quorum arithmetic explains some such examples, but a general protocol
has stateful dependencies. Several messages jointly enable one
signature; one signature contributes to multiple local quorums; a
timeout changes which proposal is legal; a commit certificate may wait
for missing block data. Assigning each channel an independent pivotal
contribution loses these interactions. Repeated deliveries raise another
problem: a search that changes a fine key's answer at each visit
implements a different scheduler from a static admission policy.

We formulate refinement directly over a labelled protocol transition
system. A finite set of fine decision keys is partitioned into coarse
fibres. Unsplit fibres share one Boolean admission value; a selected
fibre may distinguish its fine keys. Every fine key retains its value
throughout the execution. Protocol state, message generation, locking,
quorum assembly and event order otherwise remain in the transition
relation. Unequal nonnegative integer costs and allowed/required
refinements are explicit inputs.

\subsection{Contributions and assurance
boundary}\label{contributions-and-assurance-boundary}

First, we characterize the exact least refinement of any consistent
finite execution: it consists of required fibres and the fibres in which
that execution observes both admission values. This result has no
independence or quorum-additivity hypothesis. A product of protocol
state and persistent partial policy yields exact minimum-cost unsafe
reachability for every supplied finite transition system, including
cyclic systems. Its portable certificate combines an unsafe execution
with a closed set excluding every cheaper unsafe execution. A separate
checker regenerates all relevant successors from the bound input; a
search limit produces unknown.

Second, we connect model optimization to a more detailed implementation
model. A labelled forward simulation transfers an abstract lower bound
to every covered concrete execution. A concrete unsafe witness attaining
that bound proves concrete optimality, without requiring all abstract
witnesses to lift. We implement an iterative procedure: optimize an
existential quotient, test its entire fixed policy on the concrete
system, and refine the quotient using the reachable-state invariant of a
false policy. This eliminates that policy globally. Each failed
iteration strictly refines a finite partition.

Third, guarded four- and seven-validator models exercise dependencies
through shared quorums, persistent messages and round changes. The
larger instance has six controlled fibres and 48,201 states; nine
rejected abstract policies precede a certified optimum of two
refinements. A separately implemented rule engine replays the final
witness. Safe baselines, missing-support controls and every total policy
in each of three variants challenge the construction. We report total
refinement work, not just the final quotient size: refinement is not a
speedup here. The repair-graph solver remains a structural acceleration,
with weighted quota cases up to 48 pivots.

Lean mechanizes policy completion, persistent-memory updates, closed-set
certificates, local simulation transfer and invariant-guided quotient
refinement, alongside the existing graph/order and Bellman proofs. The
semantic theorems quantify over arbitrary transition systems; finite
explicit models make the algorithms executable. Application to arbitrary
client code still requires a faithful control-labelled model or a proved
covering simulation. Our checked transition bridge is between explicit
models. Separate ordinary-operation tests of unchanged CometBFT code
support selected protocol obligations.

\section{Protocol semantics and the control
interface}\label{protocol-semantics-and-the-control-interface}

Let \(P=(S,s_0,\to,B)\) be a labelled transition system with unsafe
states \(B\subseteq S\). In an agreement test, \(B\) means conflicting
decisions by the designated correct observers. State includes whatever
the protocol needs: signed messages and their availability, vote sets,
locks, valid blocks, timers, membership and decisions. These fields need
not decompose by validator. A delivery must consume an existing
envelope; those legality conditions belong to \(\to\).

Let \(F\) be a finite set of fine admission keys, \(K\) a finite set of
coarse fibres, and \(\kappa:F\to K\). A transition is either internal,
\(s\xrightarrow{\epsilon}t\), or records an actual policy query,
\(s\xrightarrow{(f,b)}t\), with \(b\in\{0,1\}\). For phase refinement,
\(f\) can contain height/view, phase and transport endpoints, while
\(\kappa\) forgets phase. A relayed vote's transport sender may differ
from its signer; certificate support must count signers. Other
deliveries and local actions may be internal relative to this selected
interface.

The \textbf{control-completeness obligation} is that all dependence on
the selected policy appears through these queries. A fixed policy cannot
also change an unlabelled guard behind the model's back. An atomic step
reading several keys can be expanded into successive queries through
private intermediate states that preserve its atomicity. If an envelope
was never emitted, an opportunity for it may stutter without querying
admission. The completed policy still assigns that unobserved key.

A total static policy is \(a:F\to\{0,1\}\). A path is compatible with
\(a\) when every visible label \((f,b)\) satisfies \(a(f)=b\). Internal
nondeterminism and state-dependent enabled actions remain unrestricted
within \(P\). Thus the objective is existential: a policy and an
execution reaching \(B\). It is not a strategy winning against every
environment choice, nor a liveness/fairness property.

For split set \(T\subseteq K\), require \(a(f)=a(g)\) whenever
\(\kappa(f)=\kappa(g)\notin T\). Let \(Q\subseteq D\subseteq K\) be
required and allowed splits, with \(Q\subseteq T\subseteq D\). Assign
costs \(c_k\in\mathbb N\), including zero, and
\(c(T)=\sum_{k\in T}c_k\). Required keys are paid even if an execution
never queries them. Costs describe supplied adapter/control choices;
they are not measured network or CPU costs.

Define \(\operatorname{Opt}(P)\) as the minimum \(c(T)\) among
admissible policies and finite compatible unsafe paths; set it to
\(\infty\) if none exists. The general semantics permit infinitely many
protocol states. Our executable input represents a complete finite graph
and a finite interface. A bounded exporter specifies the environment,
heights, rounds and opportunities it represents; its bound is part of
the theorem instance.

\section{Exact refinement without independent
pivots}\label{exact-refinement-without-independent-pivots}

\subsection{Least refinement of a
trace}\label{least-refinement-of-a-trace}

For a finite path \(\tau\), let
\(\mathcal O_\tau\subseteq F\times\{0,1\}\) be its observed queries.
Call \(\tau\) consistent if no fine key occurs with both values. Define
its mixed fibres by

\[
M(\tau)=\{k:\exists f,g,\ \kappa(f)=\kappa(g)=k,
 (f,0),(g,1)\in\mathcal O_\tau\}.
\]

Consistency remains necessary when every fibre is split: splitting
distinguishes phases, not successive uses of the same fine key.

\textbf{Theorem 1 (exact trace refinement).} A path \(\tau\) is
realizable under split set \(T\) exactly when it is consistent and
\(M(\tau)\subseteq T\). Under allowed/required constraints, a refinement
exists exactly when \(\tau\) is consistent and
\(Q\cup M(\tau)\subseteq D\). In that case \(T_\tau=Q\cup M(\tau)\) is
least by inclusion and minimum in nonnegative sum cost.

\textbf{Proof.} A realizing static policy gives one value to every fine
key. If an observed fibre has both values, uniformity forces it to be
split. Conversely, in each unsplit fibre use its single observed value,
or 0 if none was observed. In a split fibre use each fine key's observed
value, or 0 if unseen. Consistency makes these choices well-defined and
they complete a total realizing policy. Every feasible split set
contains \(Q\cup M(\tau)\), which is itself feasible when allowed.
Nonnegative costs preserve inclusion. \(\square\)

This characterizes the least refinement of a path, rather than
prescribing which path to find. The path can contain mutually dependent
quorums, alternative support, recovery, repeated messages and cycles.
Such behavior changes which paths are legal; it does not change the
policy-completion argument.

\subsection{Protocol state times persistent policy
memory}\label{protocol-state-times-persistent-policy-memory}

Use product states \((s,\mu)\), where \(\mu:F\to\{\bot,0,1\}\) records
unseen or previously selected answers. Initially \(\mu\) is everywhere
\(\bot\). An internal edge keeps \(\mu\). An edge \((f,b)\) is enabled
only if \(\mu(f)\in\{\bot,b\}\); it writes \(\mu(f)=b\). Reject a
successor if its mixed fibres, together with \(Q\), are not contained in
\(D\). Define

\[
g(s,\mu)=c\bigl(Q\cup M(\mu)\bigr).
\]

Memory only accumulates observations, so \(g\) never decreases. The
initial cost is \(c(Q)\), and each newly mixed fibre contributes its
cost once. Repeating the same admission or revisiting protocol state
does not repay a split. Crucially, merging \((s,\mu_1)\) and
\((s,\mu_2)\) merely because they share \(s\) can lose future
consistency constraints.

\textbf{Theorem 2 (general minimum-cost synthesis).} For a finite
explicit \(P\), the minimum \(g\) of a reachable unsafe product state is
exactly \(\operatorname{Opt}(P)\). If there is no such state, no
admissible policy exposes unsafety in \(P\).

\textbf{Proof.} Induction on an execution shows that its visible queries
lift to the product exactly when they are consistent and their
required/mixed union is allowed. The final memory records precisely
those queries. Theorem 1 constructs a total policy at cost \(g\) for
every unsafe product path. Conversely, every unsafe execution under an
admissible split set lifts and has \(g\le c(T)\). Taking minima gives
both inequalities and the infeasibility equivalence. \(\square\)

A priority queue explores product states in increasing \(g\). Because
\(g\) is determined by the state, revisiting it cannot yield a cheaper
price; the first popped unsafe state attains the minimum. There are at
most \(|S|3^{|F|}\) states. The implementation indexes outgoing input
edges and supports internal cycles, repeated labels, zero costs and
nondeterministic successors. Exhausting a configured state or time
budget returns unknown, never infeasible.

Worst-case exponential behavior is expected. The cost-bounded decision
problem is NP-complete already on acyclic input graphs with two fine
keys per fibre. A weighted Set Cover instance is represented by first
querying one phase negatively for each set, then selecting a positive
second-phase edge for a covering set at every element layer. The path
pays each used set once. Appendix~\ref{app:complexity} gives the
reduction and polynomial verification argument. This relates the problem
to established minimum-label and specification-revision optimization
\cite{karp1972reducibility,kim2015revision,kumar2021color}; the product
search is a generic exact procedure, not a polynomial-time claim.

\subsection{Independent lower-bound
certificates}\label{independent-lower-bound-certificates}

An unsafe witness proves only an upper bound. For a proposed optimum
\(C\), the certificate also supplies a set \(Z\) of product states
strictly below \(C\). The checker validates:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  Every state in \(Z\) has cost below \(C\) and is not unsafe.
\item
  The initial product state belongs to \(Z\) whenever its cost is below
  \(C\).
\item
  For every state in \(Z\), \textbf{every input edge} inducing a legal
  successor of cost below \(C\) leads to another state in \(Z\).
\end{enumerate}

The checker reconstructs these successors with separate policy-update
code. It does not trust an optimizer-supplied edge list or invoke the
optimizer. A SHA-256 binding identifies the embedded complete input; an
externally supplied expected input prevents substitution of another
model. The witness is separately replayed, including total-policy
uniformity and its actual paid cost.

\textbf{Theorem 3 (closed-set certificate soundness).} The three
obligations imply that every admissible unsafe execution costs at least
\(C\). A valid unsafe witness of cost \(C\) is optimal. A bad-free set
containing the initial state and closed under all legal product
successors, without a cost cutoff, certifies infeasibility.

\textbf{Proof.} Suppose an unsafe product path ends below \(C\).
Nondecreasing cost places its entire prefix below \(C\). The initial and
closure obligations inductively put every prefix state in \(Z\),
contradicting terminal unsafety. Theorem 2 transfers this lower bound to
admissible policies; the witness attains it. Without a cutoff, the same
induction excludes every unsafe path. \(\square\)

The exact reachable strict sublevel is always a suitable certificate
when \(C\) is optimal. Certificates can nevertheless be exponentially
large. Checker limits also return unknown. An omitted reachable
successor below the claimed bound, an inconsistent repeated answer, an
altered expected input or an expensive substituted witness is rejected.
Closed-set reasoning and independent witnesses follow the
certifying-algorithm methodology \cite{mcconnell2011certifying}; the
contribution is their connection to static scheduler refinement and
protocol-labelled reachability.

\section{Transferring optimality across model
boundaries}\label{transferring-optimality-across-model-boundaries}

\subsection{Abstract lower bound and concrete
witness}\label{abstract-lower-bound-and-concrete-witness}

Let \(P_C\) be a concrete transition model and \(P_A\) an abstraction
with the same fine keys, fibre map, allowed/required sets and costs. A
state map \(h:S_C\to S_A\) covers the initial states and satisfies two
local obligations. Each concrete controlled transition has an abstract
transition with exactly the same \((f,b)\) label. Each concrete internal
transition has an abstract internal match or maps to a stutter. Moreover
\(s\in B_C\) implies \(h(s)\in B_A\). More general finite internal
matches are possible; the executable checker verifies this
one-edge/stutter sufficient case on every concrete input edge.

These conditions preserve each finite execution's complete visible query
sequence. No assumption that an abstract unsafe path has a concrete
realization is needed for the lower-bound direction. In particular,
merging states may create spurious abstract paths.

\textbf{Theorem 4 (implementation-model optimality transfer).} Under the
local coverage conditions,

\[
\operatorname{Opt}(P_A)\ \le\ \operatorname{Opt}(P_C).
\]

Consequently a certified abstract lower bound \(L\) and a concrete
admissible unsafe witness of cost \(U\) give

\[
\boxed{L\le\operatorname{Opt}(P_C)\le U.}
\]

If \(L=U\), the witness is optimal in the concrete model. Abstract
infeasibility implies concrete infeasibility.

\textbf{Proof.} Simulate an arbitrary concrete path step by step,
omitting only internal stutters. The abstract path has the same visible
queries and hence the same realizing policies and required/mixed cost.
Concrete unsafety maps to abstract unsafety. Thus every concrete witness
gives an abstract witness costing no more, and every certified abstract
lower bound applies to the concrete witness. A real concrete witness
supplies the upper bound. The infeasibility statement follows by
contradiction. \(\square\)

The lower-bound direction needs coverage of \textbf{all} relevant
concrete executions. Matching one previously found trace establishes no
such coverage. Conversely, a forward overapproximation alone supplies no
concrete upper bound. This distinction allows a useful middle ground: an
abstraction may contain many spurious behaviors and still suffice to
prove optimality when one concrete witness matches its certified lower
bound.

The checker validates every supplied concrete edge, the initial-state
map, unsafe-state inclusion and equality of the scheduler interface.
Erasing a controlled admission as if it were an internal stutter is
forbidden. Our executable bridge therefore establishes a genuine
transition correspondence between finite models. The Lean
local-simulation theorem itself has no finite-state restriction; using
it for an infinite implementation requires a proved relation covering
that implementation, rather than a finite test sample.

\subsection{Refining a false policy}\label{refining-a-false-policy}

We use existential quotients to search for a sufficient abstraction. For
a partition \(h\), quotient edges are projections of concrete edges and
a quotient state is unsafe if it contains any concrete unsafe state.
Start with any partition, including one group. Solve the quotient and
check its certificate. If it is infeasible, Theorem 4 finishes.
Otherwise let \(a\) be the returned total policy. Compute ordinary
concrete reachability using only edges compatible with \(a\).

If a concrete unsafe path exists, its admissible cost is at most the
quotient's optimum, because \(a\) is realizable using the returned split
set. Theorem 4 gives the reverse inequality; replay therefore certifies
the concrete optimum. If no such path exists, its full concrete
reachable set \(R_a\) contains the initial state, is closed under
\(a\)-compatible edges and excludes unsafety. Refine every block by

\[
h'(s)=\bigl(h(s),\mathbf 1_{s\in R_a}\bigr).
\]

\textbf{Theorem 5 (policy elimination and finite progress).} The refined
existential quotient has no unsafe path compatible with the rejected
policy \(a\). If the old quotient had such a path, at least one old
block splits. On a finite concrete state set, starting from partition
\(h_0\), the procedure uses at most \(|S_C|-|h_0(S_C)|\) failed
refinement iterations before returning an optimal witness or
infeasibility, unless an explicit resource limit intervenes.

\textbf{Proof.} A refined block lies wholly inside or outside \(R_a\).
Any compatible quotient edge leaving a block inside \(R_a\) has a
concrete representative whose source lies in \(R_a\); closure puts its
target there too. The initial quotient state is inside, and no inside
block is unsafe. Thus every compatible quotient path stays safe. If the
old partition already had this homogeneity, the same argument would
exclude its supposed unsafe path; therefore some block must split. Each
failure increases the number of nonempty blocks, which is bounded by
\(|S_C|\). At a discrete partition the quotient is isomorphic to the
concrete graph, so no false policy remains. This proves the bound and
eventual completion. \(\square\)

This is a specialization of counterexample-guided abstraction refinement
\cite{clarke2003cegar}. The refinement invariant rejects an
\textbf{entire static policy}, rather than only the returned path. The
next abstract optimum cannot decrease because partition refinement
removes abstract behaviors while preserving all concrete ones. The
certificate history checks each intermediate bound, every safe policy
invariant and every strict partition refinement. The final all-edge
bridge and matching witness suffice for the optimality claim
independently of that history.

The prototype takes a complete finite concrete graph as input. Its
quotient can greatly reduce the product search, but it does not avoid
constructing that graph. Symbolic invariants or code-level simulations
could replace explicit enumeration in the theorem; they are not supplied
automatically by this implementation.

\section{Independent pivots as a structural
specialization}\label{independent-pivots-as-a-structural-specialization}

The general result subsumes arbitrary state dependencies, while a
stronger behavioral contract permits a more structured algorithm. Let
pivots \(I=\{1,\ldots,k\}\) have indivisible positive weights \(w_i\),
with \(w(X)=\sum_{i\in X}w_i\). Two certificate families need deficits
\(d_0,d_1\) beyond fixed support. Pivot \(i\) emits its first-family
signature if and only if its enabling message \(E_i\) is admitted; no
other action substitutes for that contribution. Once the first
certificate is feasible, that pivot supplies its second signature at
\(V_i\) exactly when admitted repair \(R_i\) was not processed earlier.
Initially ready closed pivots can still sign second. No other transition
changes this test; repair cannot withdraw signed output. Each pivot has
its own coarse key coupling \(E_i,R_i\): unsplit admission accepts both
or neither, while splitting permits distinct choices. In every
first-certificate-feasible execution, an admitted repair is available
and processed at its named slot. Both certificate availability and
designated unsafe observations must satisfy this coverage contract.

A finite opportunity partial order includes \textbf{all} linear
extensions, retains suppressed slots and has distinct event kinds
\(R_i\ne V_j\) for every \(i,j\). Use transitive precedence to define
\(i\to j\) in repair graph \(H\) exactly when \(R_i\prec V_j\). Jointly
delaying repairs for \(J\) is possible exactly when \(H[J]\) is acyclic.
The alternating-cycle/order-reversal proof and the finite schedule
construction are mechanized; full statements and proofs appear in
Appendix~\ref{app:frontier} \cite{streib2014dimension}.

\textbf{Theorem 6 (weighted refinement characterization).} Under this
independent-pivot contract, an admissible refinement is feasible exactly
when there is a partition

\[
I=T\mathbin{\dot\cup}J\mathbin{\dot\cup}X\mathbin{\dot\cup}Y,
\quad Q\subseteq T\subseteq D,\quad H[J]\text{ acyclic},
\]

\[
w(T\cup J\cup X)\ge d_0,\qquad
w(T\cup J\cup Y)\ge d_1.
\]

Here \(T\) is split, \(J\) unsplit and reusable, \(X\) open with first
support, and \(Y\) closed with second support. Minimize \(c(T)\) over
these choices.

\textbf{Proof.} A split key can be normalized to admit \(E_i\) and
suppress \(R_i\), supplying both signatures under the contract. In an
unsafe execution, partition the remaining keys into open reusable pivots
\(J\), other open pivots \(X\), and closed pivots \(Y\). The actual
order implies acyclicity of \(H[J]\) and the support inequalities.
Conversely, choose a legal order reversing each \((R_i,V_i)\) for
\(i\in J\), open \(T\cup J\cup X\), and suppress repairs in \(T\). The
guaranteed supports satisfy both thresholds; additional second
signatures from \(X\) only help. \(\square\)

For unit weights, writing \(\alpha(H)=\max\{|J|:H[J]\text{ acyclic}\}\),
coarse unsafety is equivalent to \(\max(d_0,d_1)\le k\) and
\(d_0+d_1\le k+\alpha(H)\). If \(Q=\varnothing\), \(D=I\) and every
split has unit cost, the exact minimum is

\[
t_* = \max(0,d_0+d_1-k-\alpha(H)),
\qquad \max(d_0,d_1)\le k.
\]

Unequal weights require actual subset support; aggregate capacity is
insufficient. The structural solver enumerates four-role assignments
within strongly connected components and combines their two supports
with quota dynamic programming. Separate Bellman inequalities certify
every local alternative, and replay supplies the matching witness.
Enumeration is exponential in the largest remaining component and
pseudo-polynomial in integer quotas. Saved cases reach 48 pivots;
configured limits return unknown. An explicit adapter also enumerates
the same enabling, repair and immutable-signature semantics as a general
LTS. Both solvers agree with an independent raw admission/order oracle
on 1,507 cost/quota/permission instances, including all 219 labelled
four-event partial orders. Every feasible general witness normalizes to
the structural admission pattern at equal cost, providing an executable
check of this specialization.

The reusable capacity \(r(T)=|T|+\alpha(H[I\setminus T])\) is not
submodular. With edges \(0\leftrightarrow1\) and \(0\leftrightarrow2\),
full quotas \((3,3)\) and costs \((M,1,1)\) for \(M>2\), both leaf
splits jointly suffice for cost 2 although neither has an immediate
gain. The only positively improving singleton is the centre, costing
\(M\). Immediate-positive-gain-per-cost selection thus has ratio
\(M/2\), unbounded in this family. This concerns that specified myopic
rule. Ten disjoint copies give certified optimum 20 versus its cost 1000
in the saved study.

\section{Stateful protocol instance and
experiments}\label{stateful-protocol-instance-and-experiments}

\subsection{A coupled guarded model}\label{a-coupled-guarded-model}

The executable rule model follows the pinned CometBFT v0.39.3 source
\nolinkurl{49b82838fcca442b2445f76605c101609ed04130}
\cite{cometbft0393,cometbft0393code}. At one height, take \(n=4\),
quorum 3, Byzantine identity \(B\) and replicas \(A,P,C\). The explicit
mutant omits only \(P\)'s round-0 persistent lock assignment; it retains
valid-block state and normal later lock updates. The baseline omits
nothing. Unsafety requires \textbf{the two unmutated observers \(A,C\)}
to decide different blocks. A disagreement involving only the defective
replica is insufficient.

The two controlled round-0 fibres are \(B\to P\) and \(A\to P\), costing
5 and 2. Fine keys include all relevant proposal, prevote, precommit and
block phases on these fibres. In particular the relayed \(A\to P\)
proposal is controlled, not silently exempted from coarse uniformity.
All other transport admissions follow the declared fixed policy. Model
messages use deterministic HMAC test authentication. The Byzantine
process supplies explicitly signed test messages; every correct vote is
emitted by a local guard and quorum certificates count distinct actual
signers.

The \(A,B\) prevotes jointly complete \(P\)'s local quorum with its own
vote. Their precommits jointly complete its repair certificate. Both
channels therefore act on the \textbf{same} quorum and persistent state;
they cannot be represented as independent contributors, one per control
fibre. Later message generation depends on proposal availability,
mixed-vote timeout guards, locks and valid blocks. The complete
environment has two rounds, 12 stages and 32 once-only opportunity
slots, including an unordered repair/precommit-timeout group and a
guarded missing-block delivery. It permits every permutation within each
declared stage and includes conditional no-op opportunities for
envelopes or timers not yet produced or enabled at that opportunity. It
is enumerated completely; a state limit aborts export rather than
returning a supposedly safe frontier.

In the minimum witness, separating the cheaper \(A\to P\) fibre
preserves the enabling proposal/prevote while withholding the decisive
repair contribution. \(A\) obtains the first actual certificate and
decides 0. A genuine mixed precommit quorum permits round advancement
without committing \(P\) to 0. With both peer precommits suppressed, the
first certificate can still form at \(A\), but \(P\) lacks even a mixed
quorum and cannot advance: absence of repair does not imply second-vote
readiness. One peer precommit can enable progress when the other is
withheld, yet complete repair when both are present. Unlocked proposer
\(C\) proposes 1; the unlocked mutant \(P\) can support it even while
retaining valid block 0. The locally collected second quorum makes \(C\)
decide 1. Appendix~\ref{app:trace} details the original guarded trace;
the coupled artifact records every action of the optimized witness and
replays each successor through the protocol code.

Repair is tested against \textbf{signature issuance}. Once the
indispensable conflicting precommit has been emitted, a later repair
cannot remove it from another observer's future certificate. Retained
controls explicitly produce \(A=0,P=0,C=1\) after such a late repair. A
separate relay schedule preserves FIFO-compatible admitted vote-envelope
order. Those timing controls use stated gossip languages and are not
executions of the production P2P reactor.

\subsection{General synthesis and checked
abstraction}\label{general-synthesis-and-checked-abstraction}

The general study compares complete graph reachability under every total
policy and every allowed split subset with the product optimizer. It
covers 7,904 graph/cost/permission configurations and six semantic
examples: repeated-key inconsistency with a cycle, a three-phase split
paid once, state-dependent cost alternatives, required unused fibres,
zero-cost splitting and a spurious state merge. These challenge
assumptions that the independent-pivot solver does not address. Five
protocol controls distinguish quorum-enabled timeout ordering, block
admission and failure of initial readiness: the same fixed fine policy
has both safe and unsafe opportunity orders, and commit waiting ends
only after an emitted block is received.

A second standalone oracle compares both direct synthesis and iterative
abstraction on 2,960 instances: 400 seeded finite systems, 2,048
exhaustive edge/cost/constraint configurations and all 512 ordered
three-set cover families on three elements. It checks 5,920 optimum
comparisons and 6,857 certificates across all abstraction iterations,
with zero mismatches. Its ordinary-reachability oracle does not use
product-state updates or a mixed-fibre cost formula; it enumerates total
policies and split subsets explicitly. Tests additionally reject deleted
input edges, missing closed-set successors, invalid repeated answers and
substituted higher-cost witnesses, and disable optimizer helpers while
checking certificates.

For the coupled protocol, the exporter is independently challenged by
128 direct protocol explorations: all 64 total policies in both baseline
and mutant programs. These regenerate successors from guarded protocol
code and compare complete reachable state and edge counts with the
exported quotient after forgetting the same first-arrival diagnostic
field. Replaying the synthesized path checks every corresponding
semantic state, authentication, actual quorum evidence and the two
observer decisions. This tests the exporter separately from optimizing
its graph.

\(1,2,4,6,8,11,14,16,19,21,24,26\) states. Its lower bound is 0 for four
iterations and 2 thereafter; only iteration 12 supplies a concrete

\subsection{Seven validators and dependent
controls}\label{seven-validators-and-dependent-controls}

The larger study uses seven validators and quorum five: Byzantine
identities \(B_0,B_1\), unmutated observers \(A,C\), supporting replica
\(D\), and replicas \(P_0,P_1\) with explicit missing-lock-update
mutations. Each receiver \(P_i\) has three controlled incoming round-0
fibres, from \(A,B_0,B_1\); each fibre couples PREVOTE with PRECOMMIT.
There are six paid refinements, twelve persistent Boolean keys and
\(2^{12}=4,096\) total policies. The environment fixes one height, two
rounds, sixteen stages and twenty-three once-only opportunities. Every
within-stage order is included, but declared multi-message batches have
fixed internal order and stages cannot interleave. The graph is complete
for this bound, not for all seven-validator schedules.
Appendix~\ref{app:scaled} specifies the environment and seed.

\textbf{Why independent controls are insufficient.} A round-0 prevote
from each pivot helps the other obtain five votes; neither receiver can
be replaced by an independent arithmetic contribution. Votes from
\(C,D\) provide mixed-quorum timeout support without supplying the
value-0 repair certificate. In round 1, \(P_1\)'s prevote is needed for
\(P_0\) and \(C\) to emit the precommits completing \(C\)'s conflicting
decision. Delivery and timeout order also matter: one fixed fine policy
is unsafe when supporting precommits precede the two timeout attempts,
but safe when those attempts occur first. Suppressing repair does not by
itself enable the second signature.

The mutant export has 48,201 states, 55,283 edges and 36 unsafe states.
A raw reachability oracle enumerates every total policy in each of the
baseline, one-mutated-pivot and two-mutated-pivot variants: 12,288
assignments in total. The two-mutant graph has 36 unsafe policies, all
requiring a split into each pivot. The baseline has 48,071 states and
the one-pivot mutant 48,141; neither has an unsafe state. Removing
cross-pivot round-0 prevotes, nil-precommit timeout support, or the
round-1 \(P_1\) prevote separately removes all unsafe states in the
corresponding complete export. These controls test actual guards, rather
than merely changing a quorum-deficit parameter.

\textbf{What certification changes.} The initial 449-group quotient
retains control progress and each pivot's round-0 value-0 prevote count
and emitted precommit; it forgets other guard-relevant evidence. This
seed was chosen after an exploratory coarse-seed run, not learned
automatically. Its apparent cost-0 violation is spurious. Across ten
unit-cost iterations the lower bounds are \(0,1,1,1,1,1,1,2,2,2\). The
first nine policies are rejected by complete concrete-policy
reachability and excluded by invariant refinement. Every one of the
55,283 input edges is checked at every iteration. Only the final cost-2
lower bound is attained by an actual witness. The guarded
dictionary-based replay checks all twenty-three successors against the
independent compact explorer, emits twenty-six authenticated model
messages and records 418 events, passing eleven audits. Its unmutated
observers decide \(A=0,C=1\).

With unit costs the witness splits \(A\to P_0\) and \(A\to P_1\): it
admits the enabling prevotes while withholding the corresponding repair
precommits. No cheaper choice can expose a disagreement in this bound.
The weighted case chooses the same two fibres for cost \(2+3=5\);
allowing refinement only toward \(P_0\) is certified infeasible. Thus
the output is an actionable interface change or an infeasibility
diagnosis, not just an abstract bad path.

\textbf{Cost of the reduction.} Direct search discovers 21,544 product
states. Refinement discovers 121,615 cumulatively, with a maximum of
21,627 in one iteration; 562 is only the final protocol quotient size,
not total memory. Its final lower-bound certificate has 7,130 product
states, compared with 7,103 for direct search. All ten abstract
certificates together contain 30,552 product states, and their
simulation checks cover 552,830 concrete edges with repetition. The full
concrete graph is built before refinement. These figures support a
smaller explanatory model and checked false-policy elimination, not a
time or memory advantage. A guard-blind 32-group seed is also run with
fixed limits of eight refinements and 10,000 product states; exhaustion
is recorded as unknown, not safety.

\subsection{Structural and native
controls}\label{structural-and-native-controls}

The repair-schema studies remain complementary evidence. A raw-event
oracle examines all 530 directed graphs with loops for \(k\le3\), 50,363
legal orders and 1,357,209 executions. It checks 103,460 fixed-split
boundaries and 35,223 weighted optimum/infeasibility certificates. A
second oracle over 48 arbitrary six-event DAGs examines 4,647 orders and
72,117 executions, checking another 3,253 optima. There are no
mismatches. These are finite encoding checks, not counts of distinct
protocols. Thirteen of 15 structural scale cases have certified optima,
including 48-pivot instances; two exceed the declared component limit
10.

Separately, four ordinary-operation scenarios execute the unchanged
native consensus package of the same pinned CometBFT revision with Go
1.25.0. They cover normal commit/missing-block catch-up, immutable
signed output, quorum-enabled timeouts, persistent locking and
valid-block proposal reuse. Four native validators produce 32 Ed25519
votes and 132 recorded events, passing 14 checks. A separate
Python/OpenSSL parser verifies every signature and its canonical
metadata; three normal repeats and one race-detector run also pass.
Source files are checked unchanged before and after the test overlay.

These native scenarios are not a native reproduction of the mutant
disagreement or a proof of the new model-to-client simulation. They
dispatch internal events synchronously using upstream test keys and
in-memory fixtures. Reactor, network, WAL and persistent signer behavior
are outside their scope. Native timestamps remain untouched, so semantic
summaries are compared while raw signatures are independently
revalidated.

\subsection{Mechanized and executable
obligations}\label{mechanized-and-executable-obligations}

The Lean 4.33.0 project checks 46 selected declarations. The new module
constructs total realizing policies, proves least required/mixed splits,
and equates persistent-memory query guards with consistency of extended
observations. It proves monotone trace cost, strict-sublevel lower
bounds, matching-witness optimality, full-closure infeasibility, and
visible-trace coverage from local labelled simulation. Its
invariant-splitting theorem excludes all bad quotient paths of a
rejected policy and proves that a spurious old policy forces a genuine
split. The finite iteration-count bound is a paper-level cardinality
argument.

These semantic proofs require neither finite protocol state nor
independent pivots. Independent kernel rechecking and an axiom audit
accompany the build. Labelled trace coverage, full-closure infeasibility
and invariant-preserving quotient soundness use no axioms; other
declarations use only the reported standard Lean axioms. The earlier
graph/order realization and Bellman modules remain integrated. The
completeness of the Python checker, the whole optimizer implementation
and the protocol exporter are not mechanically verified. Their distinct
executable oracles and negative controls support those remaining
obligations.

Earlier Boolean Lean, SMT, Python and Go checks remain available.
Agreement of 384 Python/Go rows for \(f=1,\ldots,64\) checks an earlier
terminal classifier. The historical declaration named
\texttt{bounded\_tendermint\_refines\_quorum\_pivot} establishes that
classification, not a transition refinement. The new all-edge bridge is
separately identified in the claim ledger.

\subsection{Relationship to prior
methods}\label{relationship-to-prior-methods}

Communication-closure testing compares asynchronous and lossy
synchronous executions under explicit implementation conditions
\cite{dragoi2020communication}. Its communication rounds distinguish
protocol steps. Coupling admission across those steps is an additional
operator; our negative examples do not refute its normalization theorem.
State observations also require careful treatment in action-based
reductions, as the inconsistent-labelling literature demonstrates
\cite{neele2021labelling}.

Twins already discusses certificate arrivals advancing stale twins and
provides message-type filtering \cite{bano2021twins}. ByzzFuzz, Netrix
and ByzzBench supply randomized testing, programmable execution and
benchmark infrastructure
\cite{winter2023randomized,dragoi2024netrix,louroneto2025byzzbench}.
FEST adapts schedule generation using behavior and scenario feedback
while retaining its underlying scheduler constraints \cite{li2026fest}.
We optimize the cost of a declared admission interface for an
existential safety witness; no bug-finding-speed ranking of these
frameworks is claimed.

Product-state reachability, simulation with internal actions,
independently checked witnesses and abstraction refinement are
established techniques
\cite{lynch1995simulations,mcconnell2011certifying,clarke2003cegar}.
Minimum-label paths and minimal specification revision already optimize
reusable labels or changes instead of paying per transition
\cite{kumar2021color,kim2015revision}. Once compiled into our policy
product, shortest-path optimization itself is conventional. Our
contribution is the admission semantics and its end-to-end assurance
contract: persistent fine-key answers, a one-time cost for splitting a
coarse fibre, independently checked cheaper-policy exclusion, and
matching concrete replay through a checked covering relation. The
refinement invariant rejects an entire fixed policy rather than only one
abstract path. We do not claim general CEGAR, simulation, shortest
paths, or the idea of minimal revision as new.

This distinction also identifies a practical use. Given a bounded
regression scenario, a harness developer supplies controllable fibres
and estimated implementation costs. A finite optimum identifies which
phase predicates must be exposed; infeasibility under an allowed set
diagnoses an insufficient interface within the bound. Neither outcome
alone establishes safety of the unbounded client. The seven-validator
study demonstrates this decision with interacting controls and
explicitly shows why an unchecked cost-0 abstract witness would give the
wrong instrumentation advice. The certificates make that advice
auditable even when abstraction is slower than direct search.

\subsection{Scope and reproduction}\label{scope-and-reproduction}

Optimality now covers every execution of a supplied complete finite
transition model under its declared static control interface.
Interdependent locks, alternative quorum support and state-dependent
message generation are permitted. Recoveries, changing membership or
additional phases can be included as state and transitions; the artifact
does not instantiate every such extension. Changing the interface to
adaptive decisions, dropping legal transitions, omitting controlled
queries or treating an unexpanded frontier as terminal changes the
problem and invalidates the corresponding claim.

For arbitrary unbounded client programs, a terminating complete
reachability optimizer cannot be expected in general;
Appendix~\ref{app:complexity} gives the direct halting reduction.
Nevertheless the semantic transfer theorem can cover an infinite
concrete implementation when its local simulation and cost/observation
obligations are proved. The present source-backed guarded model and
native conformance tests do not discharge that universal client
correspondence.

\section{Conclusion}\label{conclusion}

Minimum-cost phase control can be proved directly from protocol
transitions and persistent admission decisions. The exact mixed-fibre
characterization supports arbitrary state dependencies, while closed
sublevel certificates establish that no cheaper policy exposes unsafety.
Local simulation transfers that lower bound to a more detailed model,
and a matching concrete witness closes the optimality argument.
Policy-invariant refinement provides a constructive way to remove
misleading abstract behaviors. Independent-pivot graphs remain a useful
structural acceleration within this broader framework. The remaining
implementation obligation is explicit transition, control and
observation coverage for the client and execution language being
claimed.
