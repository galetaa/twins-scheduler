{\LARGE\bfseries Certifying Minimum-Cost Phase Refinements for BFT Testing\par}
\vspace{12mm}
\begin{minipage}{0.88\textwidth}
\textbf{Abstract.}
Sharing one admission bit across voting phases can remove a BFT test's agreement counterexample: additional evidence may repair a validator before it signs an indispensable conflicting vote. We turn this observation into a constructive design problem: which phase distinctions restore a witness at minimum cost? For an explicit independent-pivot certificate schema, we characterize weighted feasibility under arbitrary finite opportunity orders, unequal control costs, and allowed or required keys. A derived repair graph connects reusable contributors to jointly realizable event reversals. Component-wise enumeration and quota dynamic programming produce a legal model execution plus independently checked optimality or infeasibility certificates. A three-key family shows that the control value is not submodular and that immediate-gain-per-cost selection can be arbitrarily expensive. Lean mechanizes graph/event-order realization and the Bellman certificate principle. Two raw-event oracles validate 35,223 and 3,253 optimum certificates; structural cases reach 48 pivots with explicit resource limits. A guarded four-validator model supplies the disagreement witness. Separately, four normal-operation scenarios execute unchanged CometBFT code and validate 32 native signatures. These conformance checks support selected abstraction obligations; they do not reproduce the model's mutation or establish a full client refinement.
\end{minipage}
