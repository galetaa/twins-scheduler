Phase Erasure Can Hide Agreement-Violation
Witnesses in Certificate-Driven Locking BFT Protocols
Anonymous submission draft
Evidence cut-off: 21 August 2026

Abstract. Fault-injection frameworks for Byzantine fault-tolerant protocols expose different
amounts of control over message delivery. Some choose one partition or directed endpoint
graph for an entire view, whereas others distinguish message phases, individual instances,
or replay. A richer scheduler trivially generates more executions; the scientific question is
whether that authority changes the set of safety observations.
We study closure from phase-specific admission to one type-uniform directed endpoint graph
for finite certificate-driven locking systems. We first give a legal-target relational theorem:
closure-added deliveries preserve an unsafe terminal state when they are monotone under
a simulation preorder and the closed trace is a legal execution of the coarse grammar. We
then identify a structural boundary. If two conflicting certificate opportunities require one
correct quorum pivot, an enabling message makes the pivot available, and a later repair
message on the same endpoint and view disables it before the second opportunity, phase
erasure can delete the violation witness. For every f ≥ 1, with n = 3f + 1 and quorum
q = 2f + 1, we instantiate this criterion in an explicit bounded family. We also give a bounded
rule-level refinement to Tendermint-style Propose/Prevote/Precommit/Commit rules: under
a controlled MISSING_LOCK_UPDATE mutation, phase-specific admission exposes the conflict
while both values of the type-uniform edge bit remain safe.
A secondary result characterizes exact replay erasure. Removing a marked duplicate preserves the final protocol-relevant projection and the complete protocol-relevant output trace
only when the transition and output functions are congruent under that projection, processed stable keys are included in it, and the duplicate is a projected stutter with empty relevant output. The artifact independently checks the proofs’ finite obligations, implements
the Tendermint-style bridge in Python and Go, and supplies Lean and Coq developments. No
production-client vulnerability, universal scheduler hierarchy, or liveness result is claimed.

1. Introduction
Byzantine fault-tolerant (BFT) protocols are routinely evaluated under adversarial message schedules.
The testing harness decides which messages are delivered, withheld, delayed, reordered, or replayed,
and therefore determines which parts of the adversarial execution language are explored. Twins realizes
several Byzantine behaviors with replicas that share one identity and receive different communication
views (Bano et al. 2022). Randomized BFT testing extends the fault language with message mutation
and richer delivery choices (Winter et al. 2023). Netrix exposes programmable predicates and actions
over messages and executions (Drăgoi, Nagendra, and Srivas 2024), while ByzzBench supplies controlled
benchmark infrastructure for comparing testing algorithms (Neto and Ozkan 2025).
These systems establish that richer controls are useful. They do not by themselves answer a propertypreservation question. Suppose a phase-specific policy can deliver a PREVOTE on an edge while withholding a later PRECOMMIT on that same edge, whereas a coarser policy assigns one Boolean admission
decision to the endpoint pair for the whole view. The coarse policy necessarily admits both phases or neither. Does this loss of control merely collapse redundant traces, or can it remove an agreement-violation
observation?
Raw trace inclusion is insufficient. A per-instance scheduler produces more schedules than a per-view
graph almost by definition. The meaningful object is the image of those executions under a safety observation. Two grammars can differ on internal traces yet expose exactly the same conflicting decisions.
Conversely, adding messages during closure can change protocol state and repair an execution that was
unsafe before closure. This paper isolates the mechanism responsible for the latter case.
The central mechanism is a unique correct quorum pivot. Two conflicting certificate opportunities
each have q − 1 base contributions and need the same correct validator p. An enabling envelope E makes
p available to the first branch. A later repair envelope R, sent on the same endpoint in the same view but

The result is not a claim that graph-based schedules are universally incomplete. It is an existence and

round communication graphs as first-class semantic objects (Charron-Bost and Schiper 2009; Winkler,
Schmid, and Moses 2020). Our question is narrower: when closure from phase-labelled admission to one
endpoint bit preserves finite agreement violations, and when closure-added repair evidence destroys the
witness.
1.1 Contributions
The paper makes four ordered contributions.
1. Phase-erasure boundary. We identify the repair/pivot mechanism by which closure from phasespecific admission to a type-uniform directed endpoint graph deletes an agreement-violation witness.
2. Legal relational preservation theorem. We give sufficient conditions under which closure-added
deliveries preserve an unsafe terminal state, explicitly requiring that the transformed trace is legal in
the target scheduler language.
3. Structural criterion, parametric family, and natural protocol bridge. We state a protocolindependent quorum-pivot separation criterion, derive an explicit n = 3f + 1 family, characterize its
seven Boolean premises, and instantiate the mechanism in bounded Tendermint-style locking rules
pinned to the CometBFT v0.39.3 specification (CometBFT Project 2026).
4. Replay-erasure boundary. As a secondary closure dimension, we prove that exact duplicate erasure
preserves both relevant state and relevant output traces only under state and output congruence, projected duplicate stutter, and silent duplicate handling.
The SMT queries, finite checkers, Go/Python models, and prover sources are evidence for these contributions rather than separate intellectual contributions.
1.2 Scope
The scope is finite agreement safety in authenticated, certificate-driven locking schemas. We do not claim
a vulnerability in CometBFT, HotStuff, Tendermint, or another deployed implementation; universal incompleteness of partition or directed grammars; universal minimality of phase-specific scheduling; a
liveness theorem; or completeness under unbounded partial synchrony. The CometBFT connection is
a bounded refinement to published transition rules under one explicit mutation, not a source-to-source
proof for the production Go client.

2.4 Closure as a legal trace transformation
For a fine policy AT , closure constructs a candidate target trace C(τ ) by retaining the original protocol
actions and inserting deliveries admitted by Ctype (AT ) that were withheld by AT . Closure never invents
an envelope or changes the relative order of original actions. This sequence is a semantic target only if
C(τ ) ∈ LGD (P, B).
The legality obligation is independent of the relational preservation proof. A target grammar may forbid an insertion, a receiver may reject it, or its processing may invalidate the remaining action sequence.
The next section therefore separates merged-trace reasoning from target-language legality.

3. When closure preserves unsafety
3.1 Merged-trace lifting lemma
Let R ⊆ S × S be a preorder. Consider an original trace and a merged trace that retains every original
action in order and inserts closure-required delivery actions. Suppose:
• initial relation: R(s0 , s0 );
• increasing insertion: for every inserted action b, R(s, δ(s, b));
• forward simulation: for every retained action a, R(s, t) implies R(δ(s, a), δ(t, a));
• transitivity: R is transitive.
Lemma 1 (merged-trace lifting). If the original and merged traces begin in related states, then their
terminal states are related.
Proof. Induct over the merged trace while counting how many original actions have been consumed.
At an inserted action, increasing insertion extends the relation and transitivity composes it with the current invariant. At a retained action, forward simulation advances both sides. At the end, all original
actions have been consumed, giving the terminal relation. □

4. Quorum-pivot separation
4.1 Structural criterion
Let P be a bounded certificate-driven locking system with threshold q. Consider two conflicting decision
opportunities C0 < C1 for values 0 and 1. The following premises define the structural mechanism.
1. Each branch has fixed base support of q − 1 contributions.
2. A correct identity p is the only contribution capable of completing both branches inside the bound.
3. An already-emitted enabling envelope E is processed before C0 and makes p available to the first
branch.
4. An already-emitted repair envelope R is processed after C0 but before C1 and makes p unavailable to
the second branch.
5. E and R have the same view, sender, and receiver but distinct phase labels.
6. A phase-specific policy admits E and withholds R.
7. The directed grammar has one Boolean bit for that shared edge; all other productions, edges, deliveries,
and phase order are fixed. Edge off withholds both envelopes, and edge on processes both in the order
E < C0 < R < C 1 .
8. A correct decision for value i is emitted when the corresponding certificate reaches q, and decisions
are irreversible inside the bound.
Theorem 2 (quorum-pivot separation criterion). Every bounded system satisfying Premises 1–8
has an agreement-violating execution under GT , while no execution in the stated GD domain violates
agreement:
UnsafeObsGT (P, B) ̸= ∅,

UnsafeObsGD (P, B) = ∅.

Proof. Under GT , E is delivered and R is not. The first base of q − 1 plus p forms the value-0 certificate
and decision at C0 . Since repair is absent, p remains available; the second base plus p forms the conflicting
value-1 certificate and decision at C1 .
For GD , the Boolean domain is exhaustive. If the edge is off, neither E nor R is delivered, so the first
branch remains at q − 1. If the edge is on, E enables the first certificate, but R is processed before C1 and
disables p; the second branch remains at q − 1. Hence neither directed execution contains two conflicting
correct decisions. □
This theorem is the intermediate structural result: it identifies a checkable mechanism without tying
the statement to one hand-written quorum arithmetic instance.

6.1 Published rule vocabulary

locks or relocks the block and precommits it (CometBFT Project 2026). We use these published rules as a
natural transition vocabulary, not as evidence of an implementation bug.

7. Replay erasure as a secondary closure dimension
Phase erasure is the main result. Replay illustrates a different boundary: a repeated byte string may be
immediately silent yet alter hidden state that affects future processing.

δ : S × M → S be the transition function and
out : S × M → O∗
the protocol-relevant output produced while processing one message.

π(s) = π(t) ⇒ π(δ(s, m)) = π(δ(t, m)),

(state congruence)

Theorem 3 (replay erasure with outputs). Under state congruence, output congruence, projected duplicate stutter, and duplicate output silence, erasing all marked duplicates from a finite trace preserves
both the final relevant projection and the entire protocol-relevant output trace. Any state-based observation that factors through π is therefore preserved.
Proof. Induct over the original trace and its erased trace while maintaining equality of current projections and accumulated relevant outputs. A marked duplicate leaves the full execution’s projection
unchanged and appends no output, so the erased side may stutter. For a retained message, state congruence preserves equality of the next projections and output congruence appends equal output fragments.
The terminal invariant gives both conclusions. □
The output premise is necessary for the output-trace claim. Equal projections alone permit hidden
states to emit different future outputs. State congruence is also necessary: in the counterexample trace
X,X,Y, the second X changes only a hidden bit and produces no output, while Y copies that bit into relevant state. The full and deduplicated traces then have different observations. The artifact includes this
counterexample and a separate output-congruence negative control.

9. Related work and novelty boundary
Twins uses same-identity replicas and round-indexed communication patterns to exercise equivocation,
double voting, and state loss (Bano et al. 2022). Randomized BFT testing provides message mutation and
richer fault exploration (Winter et al. 2023). Netrix and ByzzBench provide controlled scheduling and
evaluation infrastructure (Drăgoi, Nagendra, and Srivas 2024; Neto and Ozkan 2025). ByzTwin-Range
broadens practical Byzantine experimentation through a digital-twin cyber-range (Freitas, Soares, and
Martins 2026). None of these implementation capabilities is claimed as new here.
Communication closure is the closest conceptual result. It establishes a property-preserving testing reduction for a class of consensus implementations (Drăgoi et al. 2020). Our closure operator and boundary
differ: phase labels on already-emitted messages are erased to one endpoint bit, and closure-added repair
can remove the only correct contribution shared by two conflicting quorum opportunities. Heard-Of and
closed message-adversary work already provide directed per-round communication semantics (CharronBost and Schiper 2009; Winkler, Schmid, and Moses 2020), so the graph model itself is not a contribution.
VLSM develops formal validator-system traces and Byzantine/equivocation relationships under validity assumptions (Barreto et al. 2022). Functional reasoning for distributed systems connects restricted
synchronous reasoning to asynchronous operational semantics in Rocq (Ni, Renesse, and Morrisett 2025).
These works motivate explicit legal-language and refinement obligations. PERF formalizes fault injectors
and composition for performance prediction (Zhou et al. 2026), which further rules out a novelty claim
based on injector taxonomies.

10. Validity and limitations
Internal validity. A shared specification error could affect the formal and executable routes. Mitigations
include independent Python and Go models, an independent checker, SMT encodings with damaged controls, exact arithmetic, source-level prover developments, and a safe unmutated baseline. The natural
protocol bridge is deliberately small enough to inspect line by line.
Construct validity. Scheduler expressiveness is measured by the set of unsafe terminal observations,
not schedule count, code coverage, or bug yield. A different property can induce a different grammar
ordering. Type closure is precisely the stable-filter operator of Section 2; systems that permit delay inside
a view, phase-specific exceptions, or delivery outside partitions need a separate conformance map.
External validity. The family uses unweighted 3f + 1 quorums and finite views. The Tendermintstyle bridge is rule-level and bounded. Weighted stake, validator-set changes, multi-height state transfer,
disk recovery, liveness, and pacemaker behavior are outside the theorem. The bridge does not show that
CometBFT contains MISSING_LOCK_UPDATE.
Proof assurance. The mathematical obligations are independently encoded and checked in several
routes, but only an executed mature-prover build can remove the remaining compiler-level assurance
condition for the Lean/Coq sources. The artifact reports that status explicitly.
Responsible use. All adversarial schedules and mutations run locally. No public network, assets, private keys, denial-of-service traffic, or unauthorized access are involved. A future implementation-level
finding should be privately minimized and disclosed before publication of sensitive details.

12. Conclusion
Phase erasure can change more than the number of schedules. When enabling and repair evidence share
one endpoint and view, but differ by phase, closing their admission to a single directed edge bit can insert
repair before a later certificate opportunity. If repair disables the unique correct quorum pivot, the coarse
grammar deletes an agreement-violation witness.
A legal relational theorem identifies when closure is safe; a structural criterion identifies when the locking mechanism defeats that monotonicity; the 3f + 1 family and bounded Tendermint-style instantiation
make the boundary concrete. The diagnostic seven-premise model states the exact local conditions, while
the replay theorem shows why future state and output congruence are required for duplicate erasure.
The practical implication is methodological. Testing frameworks should not equate a richer API with
a scientific result, nor interpret a smaller scheduler language as property preserving without a closure
proof. For a concrete protocol, one must either discharge the legal and relational obligations or exhibit a
