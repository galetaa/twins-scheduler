\begin{abstract}
Fault-injection frameworks for Byzantine fault-tolerant protocols expose different amounts of control over message delivery. Some choose one partition or directed endpoint graph for an entire view, whereas others distinguish message phases, individual instances, or replay. We study closure from phase-specific admission to one type-uniform directed endpoint graph for finite certificate-driven locking systems. A legal relational theorem gives sufficient conditions for preserving an unsafe terminal state. A structural quorum-pivot criterion and a parametric $3f+1$ family show the boundary: closure-added repair evidence can disable the unique correct contributor required by two conflicting quorums. We instantiate the mechanism in bounded Tendermint-style rules under a controlled missing-lock mutation. A secondary theorem proves exact replay erasure only under state and output congruence, duplicate stutter, and silent output. No production-client vulnerability, universal hierarchy, or liveness result is claimed.
\end{abstract}
