# Response to the review: recommendations 7.1–7.4

The strengthened manuscript is titled **Certifying Minimum-Cost Phase Refinements for BFT Testing**. We retain the corrected repair-frontier characterization and complete four-validator model, and add constructive weighted-cost synthesis, independently checkable optimality/infeasibility certificates, a mechanized graph-to-schedule bridge, and ordinary-operation checks on unmodified CometBFT.

## 7.1. Behavioral assumptions and the proof boundary

The first-vote enabling requirement, positive second-vote persistence without repair, timely-repair efficacy, and coverage of the stated two-certificate observations are explicit. They are separate from admission coupling and the legal opportunity-order domain. The conditional Lean theorem quantifies over execution/voting functions, and a negative control shows that persistence does not follow from the other behavioral conditions.

The repair deadline is issuance of the indispensable conflicting precommit, not the later observer decision. Repair cannot retract a previously emitted signature. The initial-ready contract also allows a pivot with a closed enabling key to supply its second-branch vote.

We now mechanize the previously unmechanized order-theoretic bridge: the selected repair graph is acyclic exactly when a full finite injective event schedule preserves the original order and places each selected vote before its repair. A cover-edge version explicitly takes transitive reachability. A second Lean module proves the extended-natural Bellman lower-bound principle, minimum-cost witness soundness, and certified infeasibility. These additions expand the audited set to 27 declarations. They do not verify the Python parser, the entire optimizer implementation, or a production-client refinement. ## 7.3. Generalization into a certifying synthesis procedure

The earlier unit-weight formula for a prescribed split set, $|S|+\alpha(H[I\setminus S])$, is retained as a starting lemma, not presented as a new result of this further revision. The new synthesis problem admits positive unequal weights, nonnegative unequal costs, and allowed/required split keys.

An exact canonical partition $I=S\dot\cup J\dot\cup X\dot\cup Y$ requires acyclic H[J] and sufficient weighted supports for both certificates. Component enumeration and residual-quota dynamic programming minimize the cost of S. The output includes admissions, an augmented topological event order, actual signer sets, and Bellman lower-bound tables. An independent checker validates all local inequalities and replays the witness without invoking the global optimizer. Suppressed repair opportunities retain every original transitive constraint.

A new counterexample shows that refinement capacity is monotone but not submodular. For two cycles sharing a center, costs $(M,1,1)$ and full quotas, the explicitly defined positive marginal-gain-per-cost heuristic pays M while the optimum pays 2. The unbounded ratio concerns this specified myopic policy, not all approximation algorithms.

The main raw-event oracle agrees with 35,223 optimization certificates; a separately written arbitrary-order oracle adds 3,253, totaling 38,476 comparisons with zero mismatches. Their scopes remain separate. There are also 103,460 prescribed-set boundary checks. Of 15 deterministic scale cases, 13 are exactly solved and independently certified, including structured cases with 48 pivots; two exceed the configured component-size limit and remain unknown. Runtime dependence is exponential in component size and pseudo-polynomial in quota values, not polynomial in binary input length.
